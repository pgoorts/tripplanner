TripPlanner Publishing Manual
==============================
This is the developer-run manual for publishing TripPlanner to your private group of testers
via Google Play Internal testing. Everything in this document requires your own Google/Play
credentials, so none of it could be done automatically — follow it step by step. It covers
both the one-time setup and the repeatable "day 2" update process (see Section 9).

Reference values you'll need throughout this manual:
- Package name (application ID): com.pgoorts.tripplanner
- Upload keystore: App/upload-keystore.jks (alias tripplanner-upload — you were given the
  password directly when it was generated; it is not stored anywhere in this repo or docs)
- Upload key SHA-1:   6E:CF:8F:97:C0:17:FC:62:4D:9C:D6:75:56:C9:91:71:E9:69:FF:33
- Upload key SHA-256: 60:0B:DF:34:60:1A:7D:BB:FD:DC:FE:D8:A5:54:07:5C:72:73:81:E0:1A:D6:31:02:B8:93:8E:EF:C3:47:F2:49
- Firebase project: tripplanner-a6078
- Privacy policy URL: https://tripplanner-a6078.web.app
- Release bundle location after building: App/app/build/outputs/bundle/release/app-release.aab

---

1. Create a Google Play Developer Account
==========================================
- Go to https://play.google.com/console/signup and sign in with the Google account you want
  to publish TripPlanner under.
- Pay the one-time $25 USD registration fee.
- Complete identity verification (Google may ask for a government ID and/or business
  documents, depending on account type). This step is handled entirely by Google and can take
  anywhere from a few hours to a few days — there's nothing to do on your end but wait once
  submitted.
- You cannot create an app entry or upload anything until this account is fully verified, so
  this is a hard prerequisite for everything below.

---

2. Create the App Entry in Play Console
=========================================
- In Play Console, click "Create app".
- App name: TripPlanner (or your preferred display name — this can be changed later).
- Default language: English (or your preference).
- App or game: App.
- Free or paid: Free.
- Confirm the declarations (US export laws, Play policies, US developer distribution
  agreement) — read them, then accept.
- This creates the app's dashboard, where the remaining setup happens.

---

3. Complete Required App Setup (Data Safety, Content Rating, Privacy Policy)
===============================================================================
Even for Internal testing, Play Console requires these to be filled in before any release —
including internal — can roll out. In the app dashboard, go to "App content" (left sidebar)
and complete each section:

- Privacy policy: enter the URL https://tripplanner-a6078.web.app
- Data safety: declare what TripPlanner collects/shares. Based on what the app actually does:
  - Personal info collected: Name, Email address (via Google Sign-in).
  - App activity / user content collected: the trip, event, note, and reminder content users
    create.
  - Data is stored and transmitted via Firebase (Google Cloud infrastructure).
  - Data is NOT shared with third parties or used for advertising.
  - Data is associated with the user's identity (since it's tied to their Google account).
  - Users can request deletion by contacting tripplanner@patrikgoorts.com (as stated in the
    privacy policy).
- Content rating questionnaire: answer honestly for a trip-planning/productivity app with no
  mature content — expect the lowest rating tier (e.g. "Everyone" / "PEGI 3").
- Target audience and content / Ads / Government apps / Financial features declarations: these
  also appear under "App content" — TripPlanner has no ads and isn't targeted at children,
  answer accordingly.
- App access: since Google Sign-in is required, Play may ask how reviewers can access the app.
  Provide a note or test account if prompted (only relevant if you ever move beyond Internal
  testing, which has no Google review requirement — but it's good to fill in regardless).

---

4. Build the Signed Release Bundle
=====================================
From the App/ directory:
    ./gradlew bundleRelease
This produces App/app/build/outputs/bundle/release/app-release.aab, signed with your upload
key (App/upload-keystore.jks). The build will fail with a clear error if
App/keystore.properties is missing — see keystore.properties.example if you need to recreate
it (you already have the real values from when the key was generated).

---

5. Upload to Internal Testing & Enroll in Play App Signing
=============================================================
- In Play Console, go to Testing → Internal testing → Create new release.
- The first time you upload, Play will prompt you to enroll in Play App Signing — accept this.
  From this point on, Google holds the actual app signing key and re-signs every release you
  upload (signed with your upload key) before distributing it. This is why losing the upload
  key later is recoverable (Google can help you reset it) in a way that losing a traditional
  self-managed signing key would not be.
- Upload app-release.aab from Step 4.
- Add release notes (can be brief, e.g. "Initial internal test release").
- Save, then review and roll out the release to the Internal testing track.

---

6. Register the Play App Signing Certificate with Firebase (do this AFTER the first upload)
================================================================================================
This is a second, separate fingerprint registration from the one already done in Block 3 —
required because Play now re-signs the app with its own certificate, different from your
upload key.
- In Play Console: Setup → App signing. Copy the "SHA-1 certificate fingerprint" listed under
  "App signing key certificate" (not the upload key certificate).
- In Firebase Console: Project Settings → Your Apps → the Android app (com.pgoorts.tripplanner)
  → Add fingerprint → paste that SHA-1 (and the SHA-256 shown alongside it, if present).
- Without this step, Google Sign-in will fail specifically for testers who install from the
  Play Store — even though it worked for you when you side-loaded/installed the release build
  directly in Block 3, since that used the upload key's certificate, not Play's.

---

7. Add Testers and Share the Opt-In Link
============================================
- In Play Console: Testing → Internal testing → Testers tab.
- Create (or edit) an email list and add your known tester email addresses (under 10 people).
- Save. Play Console will show an opt-in URL (looks like
  https://play.google.com/apps/internaltest/...).
- Send that link to your testers (email, chat, however you'd normally reach them).

---

8. What Testers Experience
==============================
- A tester opens the opt-in link on the device (or browser) they'll use, signed into a Google
  account that matches an email you added in Step 7.
- They accept becoming a tester on the page Play shows them.
- They can then install TripPlanner from the Play Store exactly like any other app (search
  won't find it — they need the link, or to have already opted in and can then find it via the
  "Internal test" link again or, once installed, updates arrive automatically like normal).
- If a tester is removed from the list later, they stop receiving updates and (depending on
  Play's behavior at the time) may lose access to reinstall, but an already-installed copy
  typically keeps working until they uninstall it.

---

9. Day 2: Shipping a Future Update
======================================
Repeat this whenever you have changes ready for testers:
1. Bump the version in App/app/build.gradle.kts:
   - Increase versionCode by exactly 1 (Play rejects an upload that doesn't strictly increase
     this).
   - Update versionName to whatever you want testers to see (e.g. "1.1").
2. Rebuild: `./gradlew bundleRelease` from App/.
3. Re-run the Block 3 smoke test on a real device/emulator with the new build: app launches,
   Google Sign-in works, existing trip loads, create/edit of trip/event/note/reminder syncs.
4. In Play Console: Testing → Internal testing → Create new release, upload the new
   app-release.aab, add release notes describing what changed, review and roll out.
5. Testers receive the update automatically through the Play Store, the same way any app
   updates — no new opt-in link needed unless you're adding new testers.

---

Keeping the Upload Key Safe
===============================
App/upload-keystore.jks and its password are the single most important artifact from this
phase. Store both somewhere durable and private (a password manager, encrypted backup, etc.)
outside of this repository. As long as Play App Signing is enrolled (Step 5), losing the
upload key is recoverable — Google support can help you reset it. Before that point, or if
Play App Signing were never enrolled, losing it would mean no future update could ever be
signed compatibly with an already-installed app, forcing a brand new app listing.
